To answer your question directly: the loop is the whole round, not any one box. No box loops inside itself. Each box runs once, finishes, and gets saved — so you can kill the run at any point and it picks up from the last box, not the start.
The AI searching in circles by itself comes in stage 2. Here the loop is plain code deciding whether to go again.
The three numbers measured at the end of every round
1. Is it still finding anything useful?
Of everything this round pulled in: how many were new and passed the judge?
300 new papers that all get dropped is not progress. Counting only the ones we keep stops that from looking like success.
Stop when this falls below about 5%, two rounds in a row. Two rounds, not one, so a single bad set of queries does not end the search.
2. Have we closed the web?
Take the papers we are keeping. List everything they cite. How much of that list do we already have?
If our best papers keep citing things we have never fetched, the search is not finished, no matter what the queries return.
Stop when we hold about 90% of what our kept papers cite.
3. How much are we missing?  — the useful one
Before starting, secretly set aside 5 or 10 papers you know are relevant. Never seed with them. At the end, check how many the search found on its own.
Found 9 of 10 → you can say the search finds roughly 90% of what is out there. Found 4 of 10 → any gap it reports is worthless, and you know it before you build anything on top.
This does not stop the loop. It tells you whether to trust the result at all.
“Go again on the part that looks thin” — what thin means
We group the papers we have by subject — the same grouping used for summaries. Then a region is thin if any of these is true:

• a group has few papers but the ones in it scored highly
• a group's papers cite lots of things we have not fetched
• a time period is nearly empty — usually the last two years, because keyword search favours well-cited older work
• a survey's own section headings have almost no matching papers on our side

The thin region is then handed to plan as the target for the next round's queries. That is the actual feedback in this loop — what we failed to find last round decides what we search for next round.
