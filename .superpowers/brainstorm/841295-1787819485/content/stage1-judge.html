The judge step

Deciding whether a paper is about your topic. This is the step that decides everything downstream.

What screen_relevant() does today
It pastes the titles of the first 40 papers into one prompt and asks which numbers are relevant.

• It never sees the abstract. A title like “Towards greener infrastructure” is unjudgeable.
• Everything past the 40th paper is silently ignored.
• All 40 are judged together, so they affect each other, and one wrong index number quietly deletes the wrong paper.
• No reason is recorded. A dropped paper leaves no trace at all.
• If the AI call fails it keeps everything; if the AI returns nothing it also keeps everything. Two very different situations, same result.
• It is told to be strict. For gap finding that is backwards — see below.
What it should do — cheap first, AI only where it is needed
Step 1 — free sort
Score every paper against the topic by meaning, on your own machine. No AI call, no cost.

Clearly unrelated ones are dropped here. Hundreds of papers, seconds, nothing spent.
Step 2 — AI reads the unsure middle
Only papers in the grey band get an AI call. One paper per call, title and abstract, so papers cannot affect each other.

It answers a fixed checklist, not a vibe.
Step 3 — three outcomes
Keep — into the pile, and we follow its references
Drop — with a written reason, kept on record
Unsure — goes to you at the approval gate
The checklist — written before the run starts, same for every paper
1. Subject. Is it about data centre or server room cooling and thermal behaviour?
2. Type. Does it report a measurement, experiment, simulation or model? (drops opinion pieces and marketing)
3. Outcome. Does it report something we care about — energy, PUE, temperature, cost?
4. Overlap. Is it the same work as a paper we already hold, in a longer or shorter form?

Each answer is yes, no, or cannot tell from the abstract — recorded separately. That is the difference between “the AI said no” and “it failed on question 2, and here is the sentence it used”.
Three worked examples
“Rear-door heat exchangers in a 400 kW server room”
meaning score 0.71 → grey band → AI reads it
subject yes · type yes · outcome yes · overlap no
KEEP — and follow its references
“Thermal management of EV battery packs”
meaning score 0.34 → below the floor
DROP — no AI call spent, reason recorded
“Towards greener infrastructure” (no abstract)
nothing to judge on
UNSURE — you decide at the gate
Lean towards keeping. Here is why.

Keeping a junk paper costs a fraction of a cent and some noise.

Dropping a good paper creates a fake gap. Stage 2 will announce nobody has done something that somebody has already published.

And it is worse than losing one paper — we only follow references from papers we kept. Drop one, and you lose everything it cites and everything citing it. One mistake removes a whole branch.

So the checklist is set to keep anything it cannot confidently rule out. The word strict comes out of the prompt.

How do we know the judge is any good?
Once: your team labels 50 papers by hand — keep or drop. That set never changes. Every time the judge changes, run it against those 50. You get a real number instead of a feeling.

Every run, free: count how many papers the judge dropped that you put back at the approval gate. That is the number that matters, because those are the fake gaps you were about to be handed.

Both numbers are also what the system tunes itself on later — the answer key you produce just by using it.
